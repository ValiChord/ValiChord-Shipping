Was she an arrived ship?

A carrier's claimed arrival time, sealed before disclosure and then checked against the public record of where the vessel physically was.

Telemetry is real. CSL SPIRIT’s complete track — 460 position fixes — is unmodified public AIS from NOAA / MarineCadastre.

The four parties are synthetic. No shipping line, port authority or terminal supplied operational records. The carrier’s claim is deliberately constructed to test the method. Nothing here says any real carrier misreported anything.

Each claimed time was stated independently of the track. The port authority’s and terminal operator’s times are fixed clock values with plausible reporting lags, not values copied from the telemetry — so their agreement below is a result, not an artefact of how the scenario was built. Every assertion tested is printed against its verdict.

Finding

The carrier sealed a claim that the vessel had arrived at 05:30 UTC. At that moment the public track puts her 17.35 nm from the port reference point — 5.35 nm outside the 12 nm limit — making 12.2 knots, broadcasting her own status as “under way using engine”. She crossed the limit 30 minutes later.

Each party sealed a hash of its record, signed with an Ed25519 key, before any record was disclosed. Altering a record after sealing breaks the hash; recomputing the hash breaks the signature. Both were tested against three substitution attacks and refused all three.

What this record does not decide

  • Whether Notice of Readiness was validly tendered. That turns on whether the vessel was an 'arrived ship', a contested question of law, not of position.
  • Whether any laytime or demurrage consequence follows, and in what amount.
  • Whether any divergence was deliberate. Nothing here speaks to intent.
  • Commitment ordering relative to any external clock. Phase 1 has no external witness; commit and reveal occur in one process. See docs/09 Phase 3.
